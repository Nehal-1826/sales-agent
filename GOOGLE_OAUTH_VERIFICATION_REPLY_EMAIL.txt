Hello,

Thank you for the detailed review and for citing the specific requirement.
We have re-examined our product against the "Appropriate access to and use
of Google Gmail APIs" policy and made two categories of changes: we removed
the restricted scopes entirely, and we removed the product behaviours most
likely to read as "circumventing filters". Point by point:

1. SCOPES REDUCED TO gmail.send ONLY (restricted scopes removed)

We have removed these scopes from our OAuth consent screen and our product:

  - https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.modify  (REMOVED)
  - https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.labels  (REMOVED)

We now request ONLY:

  - https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.send

plus openid / email / profile for sign-in. The features those restricted
scopes powered — applying labels to messages, marking messages read,
starring, and moving messages out of the Spam folder — have been removed
from the Google-API code path entirely. Our application no longer reads,
modifies, labels, or moves any message in a user's mailbox through the
Gmail API, and never marks anything as not-spam on the user's behalf.
We acknowledge that programmatically reversing Gmail's spam decisions was
the wrong use of gmail.modify, and it is gone.

2. WHY gmail.send IS NECESSARY AND SUFFICIENT FOR THE FEATURE

WarmPulse is a deliverability tool for a business user's OWN single
mailbox. The feature that needs gmail.send: the user connects their own
mailbox and WarmPulse sends a small, fixed, user-configured number of
plain-text messages per day (ramping 5/day up to at most 50/day over
weeks) on the user's behalf, so the user can see whether their mail is
being delivered, and their new mailbox or domain builds a normal sending
history before they use it for real business mail. Sending on the user's
behalf from their own account is the entire feature; a narrower scope
cannot send mail, and we do not request the broader
https://mail.google.com/ scope because we do not use SMTP with OAuth.

Recipients: messages go ONLY to monitoring inboxes operated by WarmPulse
that exist to receive them, or to the user themselves. There is no feature
to send to arbitrary third parties, no campaigns, no bulk sending, no
marketing content, and no attachments. Volume is capped per mailbox per
day by the product itself, well under any account limitation — this is
the opposite of "bypassing Gmail account limitations": we deliberately
keep each connected account's volume LOW and human-paced.

3. CHANGES TO THE "MULTIPLE ACCOUNTS" CONCERN

Our monitoring inboxes are ordinary mailbox accounts that receive the
user's test messages, open them as a human would read mail, and (now) do
nothing else via the Gmail API. To remove even the appearance of
coordinated multi-account behaviour on Google's platform, the inbound
side of our product no longer uses the Gmail API AT ALL: our monitoring
inboxes are accessed over standard IMAP, like any mail client, and Gmail
users who prefer not to grant OAuth at all can connect their mailbox via
an app password over standard SMTP/IMAP — that path is now our DEFAULT
and recommended connect flow and uses no Google API in any form.
